# Workforce — independent constructed case
Wholly fictional input and reviewed expected response, not a recorded model execution.

## Input
A lunch stall asks why starters leave. It has six workers. On 2026-11-11, records show: starter A
joined 2026-05-05 and left after 110 days; B joined 2026-06-02 and left after 18 days; C joined
2026-09-22 and remains employed; D joined 2026-10-20 and remains employed. Reasons for departures
are unknown. Owner says hourly pay matches nearby food shops but hours vary. One worker needs
school-pickup availability; another prefers early shifts for transport. Ages are not supplied.
Existing tools: paper rota and payroll records. Manager can spare 30 minutes this week, no extra
cash budget yet. Training arrangements have not been described.

## Expected response
**Measured, within the constructed case:** dates/tenures from the supplied records.
**Calculated:** as of 2026-11-11, only A and B have start dates allowing full 90-day follow-up.
A survived day 90 despite leaving later; B did not. Retention = 1/2 = 50%; early exits = 1/2 = 50%.
C and D are pending, not successes or failures. Two eligible hires cannot support a stable forecast.
This is not the fraction of leavers with short tenure or the share currently employed.

**Owner-reported:** variable hours and matching nearby hourly rates. **Unknown:** reasons for leaving,
training, tips, guaranteed hours, treatment, workload, commute costs and competing-sector offers.
**Inferred:** predictability or total compensation might matter; early departure does not prove
onboarding failure. Matching a few food shops does not rule out pay. Do not infer availability,
responsibility or preference from age, or request diagnoses to explain schedule needs.

**One proportionate next check:** manager reviews what was promised about hours against the rota and
asks current workers voluntarily whether the actual pattern fits their stated availability. No
contact with former staff or sharing of employment details is authorized by this example.
Setup: 10 minutes reviewing records; recurring/check conversations: up to 20 minutes this week,
only if staff time is available and paid as required. At a constructed manager opportunity rate of
£16/hour, manager time is 0.5 × £16 = £8, not free. Any extra paid cover or staff consultation cost
is unknown and must fit an agreed budget before proceeding. Do not displace essential service.

Measure promise/rota discrepancies and voluntary feedback, not a promised retention uplift. Existing
baseline is unknown. Review after the check, with no invented calendar commitment. If arrangements
match needs, no change may be warranted; conflicting or sparse feedback can remain inconclusive.
A later scheduling change needs staff agreement, coverage costing, a baseline and longer follow-up;
any onboarding proposal first needs evidence about training. Preserve commitments and necessary
support if reversing a trial. Do not claim causality after simultaneous pay/schedule/manager changes.

Keep records access-limited; even initials and small-group summaries can identify real workers, so
use these invented labels only in this public fixture. Never publish real personnel histories.
W01–W06 supply questions, not a diagnosis or transferable turnover rate. Employment entitlements,
paid consultation, scheduling and accommodations need current qualified UK guidance, not invented
legal advice. Unsafe treatment should be addressed rather than left in place for an experiment.
