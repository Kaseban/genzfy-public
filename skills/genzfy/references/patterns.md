# Gen Z F&B behavior patterns: bounded evidence and practical hypotheses

Gen Z is an audience lens, not a stable personality or a substitute for a local segment.
Cross-sectional age comparisons do not isolate age, period and cohort effects. Define the audience
by life stage, budget, location and occasion as well as age. Never invent a statistic.

## How to use this reference

- Each P01–P16 ID is stable for other skills. The label is an index, not proof of the claim.
- `applies_to` indicates typical operational relevance, not a rigid exclusion or customer demand.
  Include a pattern for any mixed format with the relevant service, such as pickup at a sit-down
  venue. Match the actual occasion and channel rather than the venue label alone.
- **Evidence** separates reported findings from inference. **Test** is a practical hypothesis,
  not a research result or a mandatory intervention.
- [claim-ledger.md](claim-ledger.md) is the maintained evidence record. Every retained numerical
  claim is recorded there. Removed precision stays quarantined until verified.
- Assess provenance, instrument, sampling, sponsor, recency, geography, denominator, comparator,
  study design and measured outcome separately. Publisher prestige or a T1/T2 label is not enough.
- Confidence is claim-specific. Missing methods limit inference even if a press release is authentic.
  Self-report is not observed purchase; association is not causality; platform users are not everyone.
- Foreign evidence can motivate a local hypothesis. Neither direction nor magnitude necessarily
  transfers. A matching country still requires matching audience, occasion and outcome.
- Link availability and evidence validity are distinct. A blocked request is not a dead source;
  a working page does not verify its methods. No fresh URL validation is claimed in this revision.
- For content, use audience, journey, capacity and measurement to choose a channel. Do not assign
  TikTok to moving food or Instagram to rooms by rule.
- Pause marketing for a broken purchase/fulfillment path or unresolved safety, factual or legal risk.
  A weak visual score is a test opportunity, not a launch veto.

## Patterns

### P01 · off-premise-service
**Working proposition:** Off-premise deserves its own service design.

- `applies_to`: takeaway, sit-down, cafe, bakery, truck, delivery-only
- `market`: US; Canadian and Euromonitor sources require separate populations
- **Evidence:** NRA reports off-premise use and preferences, including some generational splits. Its all-customer restaurant traffic measures are not Gen Z measures. Public summaries do not establish a universal default for every venue.
- **Test:** Test pickup staging, accurate handoff promises and travel quality when local order mix warrants it. Do not infer that takeaway is the primary product for a sit-down venue.
- **Limits:** Euromonitor’s previously quoted 78% takeaway and 68% delivery figures have unverified geography here. Do not label them European or use them as local estimates. Canadian sources are retained for rechecking, not as interchangeable evidence.

### P02 · packaging-is-product
**Working proposition:** Packaging can protect travel quality.

- `applies_to`: takeaway, delivery-only, cafe, bakery, truck
- `market`: US; UK all-age survey
- **Evidence:** NRA reports stated packaging preferences among off-premise consumers, not a Gen Z-specific willingness-to-pay result. The UK CCS McLays survey is vendor-commissioned and all-age; sample details are incomplete in the retained record.
- **Test:** Test containers on the actual route and holding time, measure leaks, temperature, waste and complaints, and compare packaging cost with contribution. Do not assume packaging is the cheapest lever or that stated willingness becomes paid demand.
- **Limits:** UK delivery-quality concerns do not validate a local packaging price premium. Separate delivery from customer pickup.
- **Claim record:** [C02](claim-ledger.md#c02)
- **Source leads retained:**

### P03 · social-discovery
**Working proposition:** Social can contribute to discovery.

- `applies_to`: all
- `market`: US sources; other source geography requires verification
- **Evidence:** The retained Toast and eMarketer sources describe social discovery or self-reported restaurant action. Their different user bases and outcomes do not establish that social replaces search, maps or recommendations.
- **Comparative evidence (bounded):** Deloitte’s US 2025 Digital Media Trends survey reports 63% of Gen Z versus 49% of millennials selecting social-media ads/reviews among the media most influential to their purchases (up to two responses). This is broad purchasing influence, not a restaurant-discovery or conversion rate.
- **Test:** Choose a channel from reachable local audience, search intent, occasion and purchase journey. Keep maps, hours and the ordering destination usable. Attribute visits or orders where possible rather than equating views with sales.
- **Limits:** Deliverect recommendations research is not a Europe-wide result without confirming its countries and sample. A finding about trust cannot establish a fixed social-discovery/friends-decision sequence.
- **Claim record:** [C03](claim-ledger.md#c03)
- **Source leads retained:**

### P04 · content-participation
**Working proposition:** Do not assume customers will distribute your content.

- `applies_to`: all
- `market`: US Segmanta; UK Ofcom is general social use
- **Evidence:** Segmanta describes a US sample of more than 2,500 people aged 13–24. Field dates and a matched millennial comparison are absent from the retained record. Its food-posting results do not establish a timeless Gen Z posting rate or prove that public posting moved to private channels. The fb101 84% claim lacks traceable original methodology here and is quarantined.
- **Test:** Budget a realistic production and distribution plan. Owner, staff, paid creator and voluntary customer content are different options with different labor costs, consent, usage rights and disclosure obligations. UGC can be tested when participation is plausible and measured; neither require nor prohibit it categorically.
- **Limits:** Ofcom measures general posting, sharing or commenting, not food posting. It cannot confirm that food-content consumption exceeds publication or predict restaurant UGC performance. Do not compare unrelated millennial food-photo and Gen Z posting surveys.
- **Claim record:** [C04](claim-ledger.md#c04)
- **Source leads retained:**

### P05 · usable-ordering-paths
**Working proposition:** Offer a usable digital path where customers need one.

- `applies_to`: takeaway, sit-down, cafe, delivery-only, truck
- `market`: US NRA; local channel mix unknown
- **Evidence:** NRA’s 2025 release reports mobile-ordering use of 74% for millennials and 65% for Gen Z adults. This comparison does not support Gen Z exceptionalism: the millennial share is higher. NRA reports technology preferences with different populations across questions. All-customer delivery responses must not be relabelled Gen Z, and use does not mean exclusive preference.
- **Test:** Test an accessible mobile path with clear prices, modifiers and confirmation; retain human help. Compare direct and aggregator flows by customer need and economics rather than defaulting to building an app.
- **Limits:** European aggregator dominance or US first-party dominance requires market-specific evidence; neither is assumed here.
- **Claim record:** [C05](claim-ledger.md#c05)
- **Source leads retained:**
  - https://restaurant.org/research-and-media/media/press-releases/from-trend-to-transformation-off-premises-dining-now-essential-for-restaurant-consumers,-operators/

### P06 · call-aversion
**Working proposition:** Avoid making calls the only route.

- `applies_to`: all
- `market`: UK Uswitch/Opinium; original US attribution unverified
- **Evidence:** The UK Uswitch/Opinium survey covers adults, with a young-adult age band that is not a clean Gen Z cohort. Preferences about personal voice calls are not observed restaurant booking abandonment. The original UNiDAYS/Nextiva instrument was not located.
- **Test:** Provide a monitored written or online route alongside phone support when feasible. Publish response times. Allergy or safety-critical questions require a reliable staff process, not an unattended DM inbox.
- **Limits:** Do not quote the old US hang-up percentage or infer anxiety, abandonment or continental-European behavior from the UK findings.
- **Claim record:** [C06](claim-ledger.md#c06)
- **Source leads retained:**
  - https://www.uswitch.com/media-centre/2024/04/Call-me-maybe-quarter-young-people-never-answer-phone/

### P07 · speed-and-friction
**Working proposition:** Make fulfillment promises reliable.

- `applies_to`: takeaway, cafe, truck, delivery-only
- `market`: US all-customer off-premise; UK all-age delivery
- **Evidence:** NRA and UK delivery sources describe the importance of speed or delivery time to broader customer bases. They do not isolate a uniquely Gen Z need, a pickup-delay threshold or a causal effect of removing each step.
- **Test:** Measure promise accuracy, actual pickup waits, abandonment and complaints. Fix material failures; test convenience improvements against capacity and service quality.
- **Limits:** Delivery speed and pickup waits are different outcomes. Weak photography alone is not a reason to stop an otherwise truthful, fulfillable campaign.
- **Claim record:** [C07](claim-ledger.md#c07)
- **Source leads retained:**

### P08 · value-and-occasion
**Working proposition:** Make value and total price legible.

- `applies_to`: all
- `market`: US and Canada; foreign transfer unproven
- **Evidence:** Restaurants Canada reports 81% of Canadians aged 18–34 eating out less because of cost of living, versus 75% of all Canadians. This age band spans cohorts and is not a Gen Z estimate. Other linked sources discuss value or stated spending intentions, not a universal ordering of every customer’s motivations.
- **Comparative evidence (bounded):** Bank of America Institute reports nearly 7% year-over-year restaurant-spending growth for Gen Z households in July 2026, while millennials had the highest spending per household. Faster growth and higher absolute spending are different findings; Figure 8 presents a three-month moving average at July 2026, while the accompanying prose describes nearly 7% growth in July.
- **Test:** Show portion, total price, inclusions and an accessible entry option. Test response by local budget and occasion. Do not assume a lower price, a premium or a value message will win.
- **Limits:** The Canadian comparison is self-report, not a measured causal spending reduction. Do not import its magnitude or direction into another market.
- **Claim record:** [C08](claim-ledger.md#c08)
- **Source leads retained:**
  - https://institute.bankofamerica.com/content/dam/economic-insights/restaurants-and-discretionary-spending.pdf

### P09 · deal-mechanics
**Working proposition:** Test offers on incremental contribution.

- `applies_to`: all
- `market`: US NRA; student survey with unverified geography; UK loyalty survey
- **Evidence:** NRA describes stated deal interest among off-premise customers. The older UNiDAYS student survey has a narrow population and interested sponsor. Neither ranks actual profitability of discounts, bundles, subscriptions or off-peak offers.
- **Test:** Run a bounded offer only when capacity, unit economics and baseline demand permit it. Track incremental orders, cannibalized full-price purchases, repeat behavior and contribution after promotion costs.
- **Limits:** Student preferences do not represent all young adults. Off-peak offers can shift existing demand and are not automatically the highest-margin choice.
- **Claim record:** [C09](claim-ledger.md#c09)
- **Source leads retained:**

### P10 · loyalty-membership-and-repeat
**Working proposition:** Membership breadth is not evidence of disloyalty.

- `applies_to`: all
- `market`: US NRA; UK RSM
- **Evidence:** The NRA comparison is valid: Gen Z adults average 4.4 restaurant loyalty memberships, versus 3.6 for millennials and 3.6 overall. More memberships do not show that people are “loyal to none,” ignore points or require an immediate reward. RSM reports stated scheme preferences, not causal retention.
- **Test:** Test reward clarity and timing against the venue’s purchase frequency, redemption cost and incremental repeat contribution. Measure repeat visits rather than signups alone. There is no supported optimal two-to-three-visit reward horizon in this record.
- **Limits:** Use the US comparison only with its population and measure. UK scheme preferences do not prove that immediate discounts outperform accumulated rewards locally.
- **Claim record:** [C10](claim-ledger.md#c10)
- **Source leads retained:**
  - https://www.restaurant.org/education-and-resources/resource-library/get-with-the-program-building-loyalty-grows-business/

### P11 · low-no-alcohol
**Working proposition:** Test appealing no/low-alcohol options.

- `applies_to`: bar, sit-down, cafe
- `market`: US Gallup; Canadian and other regional sources
- **Evidence:** Gallup and other linked sources report alcohol-use trends or stated no/low interest for differing age bands and markets. Young-adult bands are not pure cohorts; surveys including minors are not benchmarks for an adult drinks programme.
- **Test:** Test a small suitable selection and measure substitution, incremental covers, total drink contribution, labor, ingredient cost and waste. Flat alcohol sales alongside no-alcohol sales are not proof of pure gain without a counterfactual.
- **Limits:** No/low drinks are not automatically cheaper to make or equally priced. Do not attribute all change to generation, health motives or sobriety, or use underage populations to score an adult bar.

### P13 · sourcing-transparency
**Working proposition:** Use specific, substantiated sourcing information.

- `applies_to`: all
- `market`: US Whole Foods/YouGov; EIT Food selected European countries
- **Evidence:** The retailer-commissioned US survey reports attitudes and stated willingness to pay; the EIT Food research reports sustainability concerns. Neither demonstrates realized price premiums or that naming a supplier increases purchases.
- **Comparative evidence (bounded):** AAFC’s Canadian grocery survey reports taste selected by 73% of Gen Z versus 71% of Gen Y, nutrition by 53% versus 57%, and environmentally responsible production by 20% versus 14%. Taste and nutrition outrank environment in both groups; these are grocery responses, not restaurant demand or willingness to pay.
- **Test:** Where useful and accurate, name suppliers or explain an ingredient choice. Test comprehension or purchasing response. Keep claims specific, current and substantiated rather than implying broad environmental benefits.
- **Limits:** The original Whole Foods link was previously unavailable; a trade-press account remains. EIT Food’s selected countries do not represent all Europe. Attitudes and observed spending must remain separate.

### P14 · flavors-and-local-demand
**Working proposition:** Test flavors against local demand and execution.

### P15 · channel-contribution
**Working proposition:** Compare direct and platform contribution on like-for-like service.

- `applies_to`: takeaway, delivery-only, sit-down, cafe
- `market`: Any market using aggregators; retained source concerns Europe
- **Evidence:** Fees depend on contract, service model and jurisdiction. Previously quoted commission bands are quarantined pending current local terms. Illustrative arithmetic only: on a €10 order, a hypothetical 30% fee leaves €7; €10 versus €7 is €3 or 42.9% more retained revenue before other costs, not 42.9% more margin.
- **Test:** Compare pickup with pickup and delivery with equivalent delivery. Include payment processing, fulfillment labor, courier cost, ordering software, acquisition, refunds, tax treatment and any platform terms. A direct order can cost more or fail to generate incremental demand.
- **Limits:** Written ordering is not free to operate. Do not promise zero-capital or highest-return gains, assume all platform orders migrate, or exclude US venues categorically. Ask for actual current fees and delivery responsibilities.
- **Claim record:** [C15](claim-ledger.md#c15)
- **Source leads retained:**
  - https://www.food-service.de/international/int-news/just-eat-takeaway--lieferando-restaurants-to-pay-higher-commissions-52302

### P16 · solo-dining
**Working proposition:** Accommodate solo diners where demand warrants it.

- `applies_to`: sit-down, cafe, bar
- `market`: Canada TouchBistro; OpenTable booking coverage requires verification
- **Evidence:** The Canadian young-adult survey is not a clean Gen Z band. Booking-platform reservation growth is not population-wide dining growth and may reflect platform coverage. The previously quoted percentages are withheld pending denominators and periods.
- **Test:** Allow single-person bookings and assess counter or table use against accessibility, actual solo demand, dwell time and displaced party revenue. Avoid assuming a particular cuisine or layout guarantees an advantage.
- **Limits:** Exclude venues without dine-in service. Surveyed preferences and reservations do not establish motives or incremental revenue.
- **Claim record:** [C16](claim-ledger.md#c16)

## Excluded shortcuts

Do not claim a universal attention span, universal spending budget, guaranteed free customer
marketing, fixed slang expiry date or optimal loyalty horizon. Do not revive a statistic merely
because a high-profile publisher repeats it. Verify the original population, question and outcome.
