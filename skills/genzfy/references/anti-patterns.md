# Anti-patterns: practical risks, not cohort stereotypes

Use these stable trap IDs to explain a recommendation. They are operating judgments and creative
defaults, not findings about every young customer. Read `references/patterns.md` and `references/claim-ledger.md` before
citing research. A trap is a prompt to examine evidence and context, not an automatic veto.

### A01 · mascot voice
A borrowed character voice that the team cannot sustain or that misrepresents the business.
**Instead:** default to a credible owner or team voice. A deliberate character can work when it
fits the audience, is honestly presented and has a sustainable production plan. Size alone does
not determine whether a brand can use humor or a persona.

### A02 · forced slang
Using slang because a demographic stereotype says it is required. There is no fixed slang expiry
period, and ordinary language can also feel forced.
**Instead:** default to plain language. Allow familiar local language, humor or slang when the
speaker genuinely uses it, the audience understands it and the message remains clear. Test the
execution rather than imposing an absolute ban.

### A03 · pandering
Making values claims that the operation cannot substantiate.
**Instead:** communicate specific supported practices and limits. Do not claim young people are
uniquely able to detect hypocrisy, or invent a values position for a campaign.

### A04 · manufactured community
Calling an audience a community without a shared purpose or a supported way to gather.
**Instead:** investigate an existing group's needs or a credible new occasion (P12). Budget labor,
space, access, cleanup and safety. Existing groups are not guaranteed to need the venue or spend.

### A05 · discount-as-strategy
Running offers without knowing contribution, incremental demand or what happens after they end.
**Instead:** evaluate targeted and sustained pricing options against the business model (P09).
Measure cannibalization and repeat contribution. Permanent low pricing can be intentional; a
limited-time discount is not automatically better or more profitable.

### A06 · trend chasing beyond capability
Promoting an item the kitchen cannot execute consistently or sell profitably.
**Instead:** test a feasible variant with demand and cost checks (P14). A trend's age alone does
not prove it has no audience; reliable execution matters more than novelty for its own sake.

### A07 · ugc dependency
Assuming customer posts will provide sufficient distribution without evidence, permission or a
fallback production plan. P04 does not prove that Gen Z rarely posts or that UGC cannot work.
**Instead:** choose owner, staff, creator or customer production deliberately. Account for labor,
compensation, consent, usage/reposting rights and advertising disclosures. Test participation and
attributable response. A photogenic dish or mural may be worthwhile, but free reach is not assured.

### A08 · platform scatter
Maintaining more channels than the team can serve reliably.
**Instead:** choose channels by local audience, discovery intent, purchase journey, production
capacity and measurable outcomes, not a fixed food-motion-versus-room-aesthetic rule. Maintain
accurate core listings. Consolidation is an option, not authority to close accounts or delete work.

### A09 · posting over fixing
Driving demand to a broken purchase or fulfillment path, or publishing unresolved unsafe,
factually misleading or legally risky claims.
**Instead:** fix the relevant blocker before directing customers into it. Distinguish genuine gates
from optimizations: weak photography, imperfect décor or a low visual score alone do not block a
truthful, fulfillable campaign. Work on improvements alongside bounded marketing tests.

### A10 · gen z as a monolith
Treating people with different budgets, life stages and occasions as one stable personality, or
assuming a foreign survey applies locally.
**Instead:** define the local segment and measured population. Age, period and cohort effects are
not isolated by cross-sectional age comparisons. Foreign findings are hypotheses; even their
direction may not transfer. Ages 18–34 are not interchangeable with Gen Z.

### A11 · tech theatre
Adding a digital surface that increases friction, excludes customers or lacks operational support.
**Instead:** test a usable and accessible path with confirmation and human help (P05). QR codes,
apps, PDFs or phone service are not inherently wrong; assess the actual task and customer needs.

### A12 · vague sourcing claims
Using broad sourcing or environmental language without evidence that supports the exact claim.
**Instead:** provide specific, current, substantiated information (P13). Naming a supplier is useful
only when accurate and relevant; it does not establish a product's overall environmental impact.

### A13 · youth-washing the room
Spending on a demographic aesthetic without checking audience, economics or the practical problem.
**Instead:** assess transaction and experience together. Visual appeal can help a viable venue and
need not wait until every operational improvement is complete. There is no universal aesthetic
budget share or evidence-based visual score that determines whether marketing may launch.

### A14 · sober option as afterthought
Offering no/low drinks that do not meet the venue's quality, demand or economic needs.
**Instead:** test an appropriate selection and clear menu presentation (P11). Track substitution,
incremental covers, total contribution, labor and waste. New no-alcohol sales with flat alcohol
sales are not necessarily pure gain; comparable baseline demand and displaced purchases matter.

## Verdict rubric for genz-cringe-check

- **ships:** truthful, safe, fulfillable and appropriate to the audience and business constraints.
  An unproven creative hypothesis may ship as a bounded test with explicit uncertainty.
- **fix:** a concrete issue is repairable. Name the trap, describe the actual risk and supply a
  revision without pretending that a preference is a research finding.
- **kill:** the proposed execution depends on deception, unresolved safety/legal risk, a broken
  purchase/fulfillment path or an unacceptable economic downside. Explain the evidence and scope.
  Slang, UGC, weak visuals or an unfamiliar style alone do not justify this verdict.

**Review question:** can the business stand behind the claim, fulfill the offer and learn from the
result? Audience feedback is useful, but the reaction of one young regular does not validate or
invalidate an entire segment.
