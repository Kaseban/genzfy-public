# Diagnostic checklist — seven dimensions, no overall grade

This checklist is not validated as a scale or predictor of commercial outcomes. Do not sum statuses
or treat tactics as performance. Earlier 0–3 anchors and /21 totals have been retired.

## Status rules

- **Supported concern:** relevant evidence establishes a specific problem. Its cause may remain unknown.
- **No concern established:** available relevant evidence does not show a problem; this is not proof of perfection.
- **Mixed:** credible observations conflict or different channels/occasions have different outcomes.
- **Unknown:** missing, stale or insufficient evidence prevents a finding. Name the missing observation.
- **Not applicable:** the dimension or sub-question does not serve this venue's operating model; explain why.

Report sub-findings when needed; do not hide a known wrong price behind unknown photos or turn an
unknown repeat rate into poor retention. Two reviewers should be able to trace a finding to the
same observations. Review disagreements before use, not by inventing a numerical compromise.

## D1 · Discovery
Patterns: P03, P04. Can intended customers find accurate information through the channels they
actually use? Check listing accuracy, search/referral paths and evidence of qualified discovery.
A recent video is not a requirement. Distinguish visibility, qualified visits and purchases.

## D2 · Digital storefront
Patterns: P03, P05, P11. Can customers understand current products, availability, total prices and
relevant dietary information on their device? Test legibility and accuracy, not file format or
photo count alone. Do not equate appearance with conversion without evidence.

## D3 · Transaction friction
Patterns: P05, P06, P07. Can the intended customer order/book/pay and get a reliable answer, with
appropriate accessibility and allergy confirmation? Observe completion and promised versus actual
time. No app or written channel is automatically needed for every occasion; a written channel
that goes unmonitored can be worse than an honestly stated phone service.

## D4 · Menu and offer fit
Patterns: P01, P02, P08, P09, P11, P14. Does the offer serve the occasion at an acceptable total
price and contribution? Separate taste, quality, portions, price, channel fees and convenience.
Travel testing is relevant to takeaway, not a mandatory test for dine-in-only dishes. A bundle,
entry-price item or nonalcoholic range is a candidate, never a compulsory scoring anchor.

## D5 · Space and occasion
Patterns: P01, P12. Does the room support intended use, accessibility and viable seat-hours?
Quiet meals, fast turnover and lingering can each be successful. Do not require Wi-Fi, power,
communal tables or events. Not applicable to no-seating formats; mixed formats need a partial check.

## D6 · Claims and transparency
Pattern: P13. Are ingredient, sourcing, sustainability, dietary and price claims accurate and
supportable? Missing marketing copy is not equivalent to false claims. Name the evidence needed
for a claim and do not encourage publishing unsupported health or environmental benefits.

## D7 · Repeat business and relationships
Patterns: P10, P12. What is known about repeat visits, customer satisfaction and contribution over
an appropriate period? No loyalty scheme is not a failure; membership counts are not retention.
Rewards need incremental-visit, redemption-cost and displacement checks. Unknown repeat data is
unknown, not disloyalty, and reward timing must fit visit frequency and economics.

## Priorities

Rank facts that need correction and hypotheses worth testing by plausible impact, confidence,
urgency and full effort/cost. Capacity, margin and safety can override a marketing opportunity.
No issue established, no action needed, and more evidence needed are legitimate conclusions.
