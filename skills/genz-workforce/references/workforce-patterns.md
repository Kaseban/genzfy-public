# Workforce evidence and hypotheses

Reviewed: 2026-11-11. These are investigation prompts, not validated generational diagnoses.
Customer research does not establish employee causes. Sector statistics cannot diagnose a venue.

## Evidence status

Earlier versions quoted workforce figures without a verified original instrument or a matching
source. Those figures are withdrawn from active advice pending verification. Publisher reputation
alone does not establish a denominator, causal mechanism or confidence. Do not reconstruct the

- Prior claim: UK sector turnover within 90 days; later examples changed the denominator to the
  proportion of all turnover occurring within 90 days. These are different quantities.
- Sources: [UKHospitality workforce strategy](https://www.ukhospitality.org.uk/work/hospitality-workforce-strategy/)
  and [Craft Guild retention article](https://craftguildofchefs.org/news/survey-finds-hospitality-industry-has-second-lowest-retention-rate-uk).
- Audit finding: secondary briefings repeat an early-exit figure, but the original method is
  unverified; the Craft Guild article concerns LinkedIn tenure analysis, not that statistic.
- Publication/field dates, sampling base, question, denominator and uncertainty: not verified for
  the original claim. Population/market: asserted UK hospitality, not a verified Gen Z subgroup.
- Practical use: calculate the venue's eligible-cohort early exits and investigate reasons. It
  does not identify onboarding as cause. Review date: 2026-11-11; unresolved, no numerical advice.

## Intervention and measurement limits

Named trainers, first-week plans, schedule notice and progression clarity are candidates, not
universal requirements. Include recurring labor and coverage costs. Employee circumstances differ
within an age group. Use anonymized/aggregate counts where possible and do not collect private
health details to choose employment outcomes. Legal requirements depend on jurisdiction.

A 90-day cohort calculation needs every starter eligible for full follow-up by a stated cutoff,
including people who already left, and success means continuously employed through day 90. Pending
recent hires cannot be counted as successes. Calendar time to observe retention is not shortened
by running a two-week implementation trial.
