# Genz workforce — understand the staffing problem first

Read `references/workforce-patterns.md`; customer patterns do not explain employees. A large young
workforce nationally does not show this venue can recruit, and early exits alone do not diagnose
onboarding failure. Gen Z is not a uniform employee type.

## Ask for the right funnel

Start with the actual problem: applications, qualified applicants, interview attendance, offers,
acceptance, first-shift attendance, vacancies or retention. Separate these rates and denominators.
Use aggregate or anonymized records, not unnecessary names, health details or immigration data.
Ask one useful question at a time; a customer profile is not required.

For retention, ask about recent starters/leavers, dates, tenure, reasons actually given and unknown
reasons. Also check:
- Pay, tips, guaranteed hours, schedule stability, unpaid requirements, transport and living costs;
  compare a relevant local job market, including competing sectors, not just two neighbors.
- Rota notice, post-publication changes, employee preferences and availability constraints. Do not
  infer that young staff have fewer commitments; study, caring and second jobs differ individually.
- First-shift tasks, training, named support, workload, breaks, treatment and expectations.
- Vacancies, overtime and service constraints; inspect timing before asserting a feedback loop.

Do not interpret a silence as proof of no training. Ask what happens or record unknown.

## Findings, not automatic causes

For each candidate explanation label the evidence **measured**, **owner-reported**, **calculated**
or **inferred**. State alternatives and what would change the finding. W01–W06 are prompts for
investigation, not a diagnostic lookup table. Compensation remains a live explanation even when
headline hourly rates match peers. Do not claim pay is irrelevant above a threshold or use a
sector wage trend to explain one employee's decision.

If evidence is missing or conflicting, say unknown/mixed. No change recommended is valid. Do not
imply employees should tolerate unsafe treatment while a business tests scheduling or onboarding.
For employment law, contracts, pay entitlements, discrimination, scheduling requirements or
termination decisions, flag the jurisdiction-specific issue and recommend a qualified local source.
Continue permitted operational planning without inventing legal conclusions.

## Costed candidates

A named trainer, written first week, predictable rota, early conversation and transparent progression
may help when they address observed problems. Include setup and recurring staff/manager time,
coverage, cash and opportunity costs. None is automatically free or always correct. Specify what
changes, who owns it, metric and baseline, window, constraints, reversal and a falsifying observation.
Check employee preferences and whether the venue can sustain the commitment before recommending it.

## Metrics and denominators

- **90-day retention:** among a defined starting cohort whose start dates allow full 90-day follow-up
  by the cutoff, count those continuously employed through day 90 divided by all such starters.
  A person leaving after day 90 still survived day 90. Recent hires are pending, not successes.
  Record cutoff, dates, exclusions and uncertainty. A survival analysis can handle censoring when
  appropriate expertise/data exist; do not improvise it from incomplete counts.
- **Early-exit rate:** eligible starters who left before day 90 / the same eligible starter base.
  This is not the fraction of all leavers who had short tenure.
- **Hiring conversion:** offers accepted / offers made, with period and eligible applicants defined.
- **Vacancy burden:** unfilled required shifts or roles over a specified period.
- **Schedule predictability:** notice days and changes after publication; record whose choice it was.
- **Workload:** uncovered shifts/overtime/service impacts with context, not a mental-health diagnosis.

Small samples need counts and uncertainty, not a confident percentage alone. A short trial can test
implementation; a new 90-day retention intervention needs sufficient follow-up before evaluation.
Use confidential, voluntary feedback when useful rather than categorically banning small-team
feedback or forcing a survey that people cannot answer safely.

## Output and coordination

Return the actual funnel problem, known/unknown evidence, alternative causes, up to three costed
moves or checks, and measurement/decision dates. Save to the venue workspace when appropriate.
If service and staffing concerns coexist, explain the possible link, test timing and capacity, and
coordinate with `genz-playbook`; do not declare them the same problem from coincidence alone.

Use concrete commitments instead of “build culture” or “make them feel valued”. Do not promise
retention, diagnose by generation, or invent statistics from the workforce reference.
