# Genz voice — content the venue can make and fulfill

Read supplied venue context, `../genzfy/references/patterns.md`,
`../genzfy/references/claim-ledger.md` and `../genzfy/references/anti-patterns.md`.
Use a saved profile/audit when present. Otherwise ask the minimum needed about audience, offer,
voice, channel and actual production time; don't rerun full intake for a caption.

## Is promotion useful now?

Separate helpful information from demand creation. Correct hours, prices, availability and service
updates can be useful even when photos are poor. Hold a promotion if booking/payment is broken,
the offer cannot be fulfilled, or factual, safety, consent or legal-age issues are unresolved.
An audit score, quiet account or imperfect room does not alone justify “don't post”.

## Choose channels by audience and journey

Look for evidence of local customer use, current qualified discovery, where a purchase is
completed, usable assets, accessibility, staff capability and available time. Google/maps, social,
email or an existing local group can play different roles; no universal motion-to-TikTok or
room-to-Instagram rule applies. Hotel/room content and intentional long-form can work on TikTok,
but a campaign case does not forecast this venue's returns.

One maintained channel is a reasonable **resource default**, not a rule. Recommend more only when
their purpose and recurring effort fit. Listing accuracy matters where customers use listings.
Do not delete or freeze accounts automatically; suggest changes and preserve existing customer
access unless the owner authorizes them. No additional content is a valid recommendation.

## Voice and production

Use real owner/staff language when supplied. Without samples, label a draft voice as proposed.
Specific true details beat invented numbers, suppliers or histories. Plain language is a default,
not a ban on slang. Natural staff vocabulary, deliberate role reversal and owner humor may fit;
check audience/context rather than age-police the speaker or claim slang has a fixed shelf life.

Separate production models: owner-made, employee-made, contracted creator and unsolicited customer
content. Each has different reliability, labor, consent, payment, disclosure and usage rights.
Maintain a dependable owned-content plan if needed, and test optional participation rather than
assuming customers will post or forbidding them from doing so.

Set cadence from actual time, skill, budget and a named owner, not headcount. Include filming,
editing, captions, approvals, replies and measurement in recurring effort. Don't film during a
service-critical task. Offer the number of concepts the user needs and can realistically produce,
not ten mandatory posts. Each concept should specify shot/format, draft copy, factual basis,
purchase path, who/when, full effort/cash and the learning question.

## Before publication

- Verify prices, availability, dates, claims and any dietary/allergen information. Don't invent
  health, sustainability, popularity or profitability claims.
- Obtain appropriate voluntary staff/customer filming consent. Avoid identifying bystanders and
  minors; use age-appropriate safeguards and local alcohol/food marketing requirements.
- Check image/music rights, creator permissions and reuse scope. Disclose paid/gifted endorsements
  clearly under applicable rules; permission to film is not permission for every reuse.
- Include captions/legible text, accessible contrast and necessary context. Each asset should make
  sense without an explanatory thread; don't shame abstention or exploit social conflict.
- Use `genz-cringe-check` for a short creative/risk review. A “ships” verdict is not legal clearance,
  permission to post, or proof of effectiveness. Drafting does not authorize publishing.
